Ouncely Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 September 2026

Ouncely is a pumping and milk stash tracker for Android. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not do with information. It is written to be read, not to be skimmed past.

The short version. Your pumping sessions, your stash and your baby details stay on your phone. There is no account and no Ouncely server, and we never see your logs.
  • Partner sharing OFF (the default): none of your pumping, stash or baby information leaves your phone. Not to us, not to anyone.
  • Partner sharing ON: a small daily summary (today's total and your stash) is sent so that one person you have allowed can see it in their copy of Ouncely. They ask first with their name, and nothing is shared until you tap Allow on your own phone. You can stop them again at any time. Your pump-by-pump history is never sent. Turning sharing off deletes the summary.

Separately, the free version shows ads. The Google ad system collects device information (such as an advertising ID) to show them — never your pumping data. You are asked for consent first, and Premium turns ads off completely.

Who is responsible

Ouncely is made by an independent developer. For any question about this policy or your data, write to ouncely.app@gmail.com.

What the app stores on your phone

All of the following is written to a database inside the app on your device. It is not uploaded anywhere.

  • Pump sessions: start and end time, duration, left and right amounts, which side, where the milk went, and any note you add
  • Stash bags: amount, date stored, location, expiry date, status and any label
  • Baby details you choose to enter: a name and how much your baby drinks per day
  • Your settings: units, reminder interval, quiet hours, theme

When you uninstall Ouncely, this data is removed with it. You can also delete everything at any time from Settings → Your data → Delete all data. That button also turns partner sharing off and deletes the shared summary.

Sharing with your partner

This is off when you install the app. Until you switch it on yourself, nothing about your pumping ever leaves your phone.

Partner sharing lets one other person — your partner, your mother, a friend — see how your day is going without you having to text them. You turn it on in Settings, the app gives you a six-character code, and you give that code to them. They type it into their own copy of Ouncely, along with their name.

The code on its own does not show anything. When someone enters it, a request appears on your phone with the name and phone model they gave. Your summary stays locked until you tap Allow. This is not only a screen in the app — the server enforces it too, and the person asking cannot approve themselves.

How it actually travels

We want to be exact about this, because “shared with your partner” can sound like the two phones talk to each other directly. They do not.

When sharing is on, the summary is uploaded to Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore), and your partner's app reads it from there using the code. So the information passes through Google's servers on its way to them. Google acts as our hosting provider for this feature and does not use the content for its own purposes. No Ouncely staff reads it, and it is not sold, shared with advertisers, or used for any other purpose.

To make this work, both your app and your partner's app sign in to Firebase anonymously. That sign-in has no email, no password and no name — it is a random identifier that simply lets the upload happen. As with any internet connection, Google's servers see your phone's IP address while the summary is being sent or read.

What is shared

Only a short summary, refreshed as you log pumps:

  • The baby's name, if you have set one
  • Today's total and how many pumps today
  • Your daily goal (how much your baby drinks per day)
  • When your last pump ended
  • When the summary was last updated
  • Your stash total, the number of bags, and how many expire soon

What your partner's phone tells you

When someone enters your code, they must give a name. That name and the model of their phone (for example “Dad · Xiaomi 23053RN02I”) are stored with your code so that you can see who is asking and who is watching, along with when they last opened it. You will find this in Settings → Share with your partner → Who is watching, with a button to allow them, to refuse, or to stop someone you allowed earlier. A name sent with a request is kept even if you never allow it, so that you can see it. If you refuse or stop someone, their entry is kept but marked as refused and hidden from your list: it gives them no access, and it is what stops the same app from asking you again more than once a day. These entries are deleted when you turn sharing off or make a new code.

If you are the one following someone else's code: your name and phone model are shown to them, and the last summary you received is kept on your own phone so the app can show it without asking the server every time. Both are removed when you stop following, and the stored summary is also wiped from your phone if she stops sharing with you.

What is never shared

  • Your pump-by-pump history — individual sessions never leave the phone
  • Left and right amounts for any single pump
  • Your notes
  • Anything from days other than today, apart from the stash totals above

Your control

  • Off by default. Sharing does nothing until you switch it on.
  • Turning it off deletes it. When you turn sharing off, the app deletes the stored summary from Firebase. The old code stops working immediately.
  • You can change the code any time. “Get a new code” deletes the old one and makes a fresh one, so anyone who had the old code loses access at once.
  • Nobody sees anything until you allow them. Entering your code only sends you a request with their name. The summary itself stays locked until you tap Allow on your phone.
  • Requests are limited. Once you refuse someone, their app can ask again only a full day after its last request. The server enforces this, not just the app. If a name keeps coming back, make a new code: the old one stops working for everyone.
  • You can stop them whenever you want. In Who is watching, “Stop sharing with them” cuts their access at once, and the summary they had is wiped from their phone the next time their app checks.
  • Nobody can go looking. There is no way in the app — or for anyone else — to list or search other families' summaries.
  • They can only look. Whoever has the code can see the summary. They cannot change, add or delete anything in your app.
  • It locks itself after 30 days. A summary that has not been updated for 30 days can no longer be opened by anyone — not your partner, not anyone else. So if you uninstall Ouncely without turning sharing off, your summary stops being visible on its own. If you would like it removed completely, email us your code and we will delete it.
  • Delete all data turns it off too. Deleting your data in Settings also deletes the shared summary.
  • Sharing is part of Premium. If your Premium ends, the app stops sending new summaries. The last one stays where it was and locks itself after 30 days, as described above, or you can turn sharing off to delete it straight away.

The code is only an invitation, not a key: it lets someone ask, and you decide. Once you have allowed someone, they can see that summary until you stop them, turn sharing off, or make a new code.

Backups

Ouncely can export your data to a JSON file that you choose the location for. That file belongs to you. If you save it to a cloud drive or email it to yourself, it is then handled by that service under their terms, not ours. Ouncely does not perform any automatic cloud backup.

Android's own system backup may include the app's data if you have that feature enabled on your device. That is a Google service and is covered by Google's privacy policy.

Advertising, in the free version

The free version of Ouncely shows banner ads through Google AdMob. To serve those ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects information from your device.

Before any ad is requested, Ouncely shows Google's consent form. Your choice there decides whether ads are personalised, and in some regions whether ads are shown at all. Where the law gives you this choice (for example in the EU, the UK and some US states), you can change it at any time in Settings → Your data → Ad privacy choices.

Your pumping data, stash data and baby details are never shared with the advertising SDK or anyone else.

Google's handling of this information is described in the Google privacy and terms pages.

If you buy Premium

Purchases go through Google Play Billing. Google handles the payment, and Ouncely only receives confirmation of whether a purchase is active. We never see your card details, your name or your billing address.

Premium removes ads completely. On a Premium device the advertising SDK is not started at all.

When you contact us

Send feedback opens your own email app with a draft addressed to us. The draft includes the app version, your Android version and your phone model to help us fix problems — you can see and delete these before sending. Nothing is sent unless you press send.

If the app ever crashes, a short technical crash report is saved on your phone only. It is sent to us only if you choose to share it from Settings, and you can delete it there instead.

Analytics

Ouncely contains no analytics SDK. There is no event tracking, no crash reporting library and no user profiling inside the app. Aggregate install and crash statistics that Google Play shows every developer are produced by Google Play itself, not by code in this app.

Notifications

Reminders and expiry alerts are generated on your device by your own settings. Nothing is sent through a server. If you decline the notification permission, the rest of the app keeps working.

The one exception is partner sharing. While it is on, your phone checks Firebase every few hours, and when you open the app, for new requests to follow your code, and shows you a notification if there is one. This check only reads the requests for your own code. Nothing is pushed to your phone from outside, and the check stops as soon as you turn sharing off.

Children

Ouncely is designed for parents and caregivers, not for children. It is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect information from them. Information you enter about your baby stays on your phone in the same way as everything else.

Your choices

Partner sharing: Settings → Share with your partner → Turn sharing off. This deletes the uploaded summary straight away.

  • Export your data at any time from Settings
  • Delete all data at any time from Settings (this also turns partner sharing off)
  • Uninstall the app to remove everything it stored
  • Change your advertising consent in Settings → Your data → Ad privacy choices (where the law gives this choice)
  • Reset your advertising identifier in your Android system settings

Health information

Ouncely is a tracking and organisation tool. It is not medical advice and it is not a medical device. Milk storage times shown in the app follow published guidance from the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Please speak to your healthcare provider or a lactation consultant about anything concerning your milk or your supply.
